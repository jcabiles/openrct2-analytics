# Park metric conventions: operators and the project catalog

**Sources read:** 2026-10-03 (America/Los_Angeles). This report compares public operator and industry sources with the draft [metric catalog](../../../metrics/), [requirements brief](../../requirements-brief.md), and roadmap [business rules](../roadmap/park-company.md). Every web link below was read on that date.

## Bottom line

The catalog covers the core visit, guest-spend, operating-day, and same-park measures used by multi-site attractions operators. Its deliberate differences are free entry and pay-per-ride revenue, a game park rating instead of a guest survey, and game-ledger operating profit instead of adjusted EBITDA. Attendance needs an explicit counting convention, while the current operating-day count needs a warning that a simulated day is not necessarily a day open to guests. Keep the catalog’s financial definitions, add precise display labels, and treat the proposed guardrail thresholds as project decisions rather than industry benchmarks.

## What operators report

*Per capita* means per counted visit, not per distinct person across a season. *Like for like* or *same store* growth compares an eligible set of sites across periods so that portfolio changes do not drive the comparison. EBITDA means earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization; “adjusted” or “underlying” EBITDA also excludes items specified by the operator. Net promoter score (NPS) comes from a recommendation survey and is not a generic satisfaction rating.

The [TEA/AECOM 2023 Theme Index methodology](https://aecom.com/wp-content/uploads/documents/reports/AECOM-Theme-Index-2023.pdf) uses operator-supplied attendance, financial reports, and researcher estimates when individual park figures are unavailable. It also notes differences in reporting periods: North America and Europe generally use calendar years, while some Asia-Pacific entries use fiscal years (read 2026-10-03). An index estimate is therefore not equivalent to an individual park’s admissions ledger.

## Mapping to the catalog

This mapping uses the draft [catalog](../../../metrics/), [brief](../../requirements-brief.md), and [business rules](../roadmap/park-company.md), read 2026-10-03. “Match” means a comparable calculation within the simulation’s stated scope, not identical operator accounting.

| Operator KPI | Catalog status and metric | Practical distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Attendance | **Named differently:** `guest_visits`. | The increase in the game’s cumulative admissions counter counts arrivals. Guests never return in this simulation; the metric does not implement Disney’s same-day deduplication across parks. |
| Total guest spending per cap | **Named differently:** `revenue_per_visit`. | It includes ride tickets, food and drink, merchandise, and a zero entrance line. The catalog has no parking, resort, or sponsorship revenue. |
| Admission per cap; season passes | **Not applicable here:** `entrance_revenue` is zero by rule; there is no season-pass measure. | Calling ride revenue “admissions” would misstate the free-entry business. Pass visitation, renewal, and return visits do not occur. |
| In-park product spending per cap | **Named differently:** the sum of `ride_revenue_per_visit`, `food_drink_revenue_per_visit`, and `merchandise_revenue_per_visit`. | Rides are charged in-park products here. Operator buckets may also include parking, games, and premium access. |
| Food and beverage per cap | **Match:** `food_drink_revenue_per_visit`. | “Food and beverage” is the more familiar public-reporting label. |
| Merchandise per cap | **Match:** `merchandise_revenue_per_visit`. | The game ledger books on-ride photos as merchandise. |
| Revenue per available operating day | **Partial match:** `revenue_per_operating_day` and `operating_days`. | A catalog operating day proves the simulation ran; it does not yet prove the park was open to guests. |
| EBITDA and EBITDA margin | **Missing:** `operating_profit` is related but not equivalent. | The game ledger lacks the depreciation, amortization, tax, and adjustment detail needed for operator EBITDA. An explicitly labeled *game operating-profit margin* could be calculated from existing metrics, but is not in the catalog. |
| Like-for-like or same-park growth | **Match:** `same_park_guest_visits_yoy`, `same_park_operating_revenue_yoy`, `same_park_operating_profit_yoy`, and `same_park_operating_costs_yoy`. | Eligibility requires a complete prior eight-month company year on the kept timeline. An operating-days comparison should accompany the growth rate. The project owner confirmed these definitions on 2026-10-03. |
| Season length and operating days | **Partial match:** `operating_days`; season length is a business rule rather than a metric. | Each company year contains March through October. Closed months are absent, not reported as zero months. |
| Guest satisfaction or NPS | **Missing as a survey measure:** `average_park_rating` is a game proxy. | The engine’s 0–999 score, averaged over park-days, cannot be called guest satisfaction or NPS. |
| Ride capacity and hourly throughput | **Missing:** `ride_boardings`, `ride_open_days`, and `ride_popularity` partly cover volume. | `ride_popularity` is boardings per open day. Hourly capacity, actual hourly throughput, and utilization require more data. |

## Guardrails and proposed thresholds

Operators pair financial outcomes with experience measures. [United Parks’ 2025 proxy](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564902/000095017025061190/prks-20250430.htm) describes a scorecard containing adjusted EBITDA, revenue, and park-level quarterly guest-satisfaction thresholds, but does not disclose a numerical satisfaction cutoff (read 2026-10-03). [Compagnie des Alpes](https://news.compagniedesalpes.com/sales-for-financial-year-202425/?lang=en) reports that nearly 80% of surveyed visitors were satisfied or very satisfied with value for money and says it manages attendance against capacity (read 2026-10-03). That percentage is one operator’s value-for-money result, not a minimum for this game’s rating scale.

A *guardrail* is a measure that should not materially worsen while a target improves. [Microsoft Research’s experimentation guidance](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/patterns-of-trustworthy-experimentation-post-experiment-stage/) recommends setting trade-offs in advance and says there is no standard way to weight them (read 2026-10-03). Its [alerting example](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/alerting-in-microsofts-experimentation-platform-exp/?lang=ja) uses a prespecified ±1% range for one metric and a tighter range for reliability; those examples are context-specific, not attraction-industry norms (read 2026-10-03).

For this catalog, the recommendation is two **provisional review thresholds**, fixed before inspecting the results. When annual company operating profit rises:

- Same-park `guest_visits` for the completed eight-month company year should be **at least 98% of the prior year’s total** for the identical eligible parks.
- The same-park, park-day-weighted `average_park_rating` should be **no more than 10 engine points below the prior year’s mean**.

Show each park’s result, the eligible-park aggregate, and both years’ operating days. Show new parks separately; a park without a complete comparable prior year has **no baseline**, not an automatic pass. These are management review limits, not published norms or statistical confidence bounds. The 2% visit allowance reduces alerts from small fluctuations but permits some demand loss while profit rises. The 10-point rating allowance tolerates small engine-score movement but permits a modest decline and could hide a poor-performing park in the aggregate. A zero-decline rule would protect both measures more tightly at the cost of more alerts. The project owner should revisit the limits after at least two complete company years using observed variation and a stated tolerance for deterioration, without changing a limit after seeing the result it judges.

The current guardrail links in the [catalog](../../../metrics/) are observational hypotheses reviewed at year-end, not threshold rules or causal tests. A breach should be reported as profit improving alongside a guardrail decline beyond its preset limit. Weather, new rides, closures, prices, and operating days then need examination before drawing a business conclusion. A lagged association between rating and visits cannot establish that the rating change caused the visit change.

## Limits of the comparison

This is a simulation with one company calendar, free entry, pay-per-ride parks, and eight-month March–October seasons. Guests never return, so pass sales, pass visitation, renewal, and retention do not apply. Reporting uses kept save timelines and completed park-years; the [requirements brief](../../requirements-brief.md) and [catalog](../../../metrics/) were confirmed by the project owner on 2026-10-03. Version-one analyses begin with rehearsal data and are observational: associations among ratings, visits, prices, and profit cannot be presented as causal effects. The game operating-profit identity may be exact for its ledger, but it is neither a public operator’s generally accepted accounting principles operating income nor adjusted EBITDA. The simulation and calendar rules come from the roadmap’s [business rules](../roadmap/park-company.md), read 2026-10-03.
